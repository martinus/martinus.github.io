A claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

For 167 years, nobody could solve the most famous riddle in mathematics. On 20 November 2026 the mathematician Levent Alpöge said that he solved it together with Claude, an AI program. This page explains the riddle, the idea, and how sure we can be. You need no mathematics from school to read it.

Status: first checks passed, one check open Other mathematicians ran the proof through a checking program on their own computers. It passed. One last automatic comparison was still open on the morning of 21 November. No person understands the full proof yet.

The riddle

Prime numbers appear without a visible pattern. The Riemann Hypothesis says that there is a strict order behind them.

The claim

A proof that this order is real. A person set the direction. An AI did much of the hard work.

The surprise

The proof is not a text for people. It is a very large computer file that a program checks step by step.
01

The riddle: how are the prime numbers spread?

You can put 12 sweets into equal groups: 3 groups of 4, or 2 groups of 6. With 13 sweets this is not possible. Numbers such as 13 are prime numbers: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17. All other whole numbers are products of primes. This is why people call primes the building blocks of numbers.

Primes become less frequent when numbers get larger. Below 100, one number in four is a prime. Below one million, it is only one number in thirteen. But nobody can tell you by a simple rule where the next prime is.

There is a formula that predicts how many primes exist below a number X. The chart compares this prediction with the true count.

The Riemann Hypothesis says: the miss stays inside this zone forever, also for numbers with a thousand digits. The height of the zone is about the square root of X. For X = 1,000,000 the square root is 1,000, because 1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000.

A comparison: toss a coin one million times. You expect 500,000 heads. The true result is usually wrong by some hundreds, not by tens of thousands. Pure chance makes small errors of exactly this size. The hypothesis says that primes behave like these coin tosses. They look random, but no hidden force pushes them away from the prediction.

02

The hidden music of the primes

In 1859 the German mathematician Bernhard Riemann found out where the miss comes from. He used a formula with the name zeta function. You can think of it as a hidden sheet of music for the primes. The sheet contains special points, the zeros. Each zero is one tone.

The chart shows what the tones do. One tone alone is a simple wave. Add many tones, and the waves build a staircase. The staircase goes up exactly where the primes are.

Now the important question: where are these zeros? You can draw them on a map.

Why is the line so important? The position from left to right sets how loud a tone is. On the centre line, all tones stay quiet enough, and the miss from section 01 stays in the green zone. One zero to the right of the line is one tone that is too loud. Then the primes are less regular than everybody thinks.

03

Why it took 167 years, and what the new idea is

Mathematicians know a second, more simple world of numbers. It works like a model railway of the real number world: smaller, but with the same rules. This model world has its own primes and its own Riemann Hypothesis.

Solved

The model world

André Weil solved the riddle here in the 1940s. In 1969 Sergei Stepanov found a much shorter solution.

Open since 1859

The usual numbers 1, 2, 3, …

The plan for decades: copy the solution from the model world. It did not work, because one tool does not exist here.

The tool that was missing

The tool is a method to count one point more than one time. The pictures show the idea. A line can go through a point. It can also only touch the point, or stay flat on it. Mathematicians then count the point one, two or three times.

The new idea: a long chain

In the model world, the multiple count is easy to get. For the usual numbers, nobody knew how to get it. The note says that a tool from a very different area of mathematics can do the job. Its name is theta lift. Each use of the tool adds one more count. So you use it again and again, as links in a chain.

How long must the chain be? Claude proposed the square root of X. The author writes that this one line will astonish other mathematicians. His own comment in the note: “And yet, what else could it be?”

A limit of this page: the note does not explain the proof. This section gives the idea in the words of the note. The author also writes that he is not an expert in this part of mathematics. Here the AI knew more than he did.

04

Six weeks, one person, one AI

The note lists 20 steps with dates. The work started on 6 October and ended on 16 November 2026.

Levent Alpöge is a mathematician at Anthropic, the company that makes Claude. He writes that he prepared for this riddle since his student days. He selected the direction and the ideas to combine.
Claude did much of the technical work, mainly at the end. The author writes that the AI was far ahead of him in that phase and that it made important proposals.
Together they wrote the proof in a form that a computer can check. The author says openly that he does not yet understand all parts of it.
05

Can you trust a proof that no person has read?

The proof is a computer file with about 175,000 parts. A checking program with the name Lean examines each step. Think of a very strict auditor. The auditor does not understand the business. But the auditor recalculates each line and accepts nothing without a reason.

Such a check can still go wrong in two ways. The file can prove a different, easier claim than the real riddle. Or it can contain a hidden shortcut: a step that it assumes but does not prove. So other people must examine these points.

  • PassedThe check works on other computers.Andrew Sutherland of the university MIT and other researchers ran it independently. Scientific American reports no problems.
  • PassedNo hidden shortcuts.Sutherland says that the proof uses only the three basic assumptions that all of mathematics uses. He puts the chance of a trick at “approximately zero”.
  • OpenThe file proves the real riddle.It looks correct. A last automatic comparison was still open on the morning of 21 November.
  • Not yetPeople understand the proof.Nobody can explain today why it works. The author expects that shorter and clearer versions will follow.
  • Not yetA text that people can read.A printed version would have more than 250,000 pages. The author decided not to publish that.
  • Not yetThe prize of 1 million dollars.The rules of the Clay institute require a publication and then a waiting time of two years or more.

The short answer: the proof is very probably correct. Scientific American uses the words “almost certainly true” in its headline. But checked is not the same as understood. The next task for mathematicians is to find out why the proof works.

06

What this means for you

  • Your bank card stays safe. Encryption on the internet uses prime numbers. But the proof gives no method to break it. It only confirms what experts already assumed.
  • Nothing changes in daily life tomorrow. This is basic knowledge about numbers. Its effect is inside mathematics: many hundreds of results started with the words “if the Riemann Hypothesis is true”. These results now become facts.
  • The method of work is the real news. An AI did a large part of the hardest work on the hardest known riddle. And a machine confirmed the result before a person understood it. That is new.
  • Mathematicians have mixed feelings. The author writes that he lost a guiding light of his life. He also writes that he sees the start of something much larger for all of science.
07

Short glossary

Prime number
A whole number larger than 1 that you cannot write as a product of two smaller numbers.
Zeta function
The formula of Riemann that connects all primes. In this page: the sheet of music.
Zero
A point where the zeta function has the value 0. In this page: one tone.
Lean
A program that checks a mathematical proof step by step. In this page: the auditor.
Generalized Riemann Hypothesis
A larger version of the riddle for a full family of similar formulas. The note says that the proof solves this version too.
